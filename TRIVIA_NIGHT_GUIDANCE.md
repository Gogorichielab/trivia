# St. Peter Trivia Night — Guidance

## Event Goal

Create a fun, welcoming trivia night for St. Peter Evangelical Lutheran Church that works well for a broad, cross-generational audience, including participants up to about age 80.

The game should feel more like a **game show** than a test. Questions should reward teams with a mixture of ages, interests, and knowledge.

## Game Length

Target runtime: **approximately 60 minutes**.

Plan for:

- **35–40 scored questions**
- **1 surprise Pastor/sermon bonus question**
- **1 final question**

Normal questions should generally take about 60–75 seconds for reading, team discussion, answering, and revealing the answer. Allow additional time for music questions, scoreboard updates, and the final round.

## Technology

The trivia application will be developed in this repository:

**Gogorichielab/trivia**

The game will run from a laptop and be displayed on the church TVs/projector.

The application should support:

- Large, high-contrast question and answer text.
- A clean audience-facing presentation suitable for TVs/projectors.
- Simple host controls for advancing questions and revealing answers.
- A visible scoreboard.
- Team score tracking.
- Image-based questions.
- Music/audio questions where practical.
- A presentation mode that does not expose answers or host controls to players.

## Recommended Question Mix

| Category | Target | Guidance |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Music — 1960s–1990s | 12 | Spread questions across all four decades. |
| Movies & TV | 7–8 | Use recognizable movies, actors, characters, and television. |
| Sports | 5–6 | Mix national sports with some Chicago-area sports. |
| Current Events | 5–6 | Events must be from 2025 through the event date. |
| General / Fun Trivia | 5–7 | History, geography, food, everyday knowledge, and light pop culture. |
| Pastor Surprise Question | 1 bonus | Based on the Pastor's sermon. |
| Final Question | 1 | End the game with a dramatic final question; optionally allow a wager. |

The exact distribution may change while developing the question set.

## Music

Music will be one of the featured parts of the game.

Music questions are limited to:

- 1960s
- 1970s
- 1980s
- 1990s

Keep the decades reasonably balanced so different generations have opportunities to contribute.

Possible question formats include:

- Name the artist.
- Name the song.
- Identify the decade.
- Recognize a song from a short audio clip.
- Connect a famous song to a movie, performer, or cultural event.

When audio is used, test playback from the presentation laptop through the church's sound system before trivia night.

## Movies & Television

Choose movies and television that have broad recognition across generations.

Good subjects include major films, well-known actors, recognizable characters, classic television, famous movie moments, and popular franchises.

Avoid making the category dependent on obscure fandom knowledge.

## Sports

Sports questions should cover recognizable sporting events, athletes, teams, and records.

Some questions can have a **Chicago-area connection**, including teams such as the Bears, Cubs, White Sox, Bulls, and Blackhawks, while still including national sports and major sporting events.

Avoid making the sports portion too specialized.

## Current Events

Current-events questions must cover events occurring from **2025 through the date of the trivia event**.

Favor widely recognizable topics such as:

- Entertainment
- Science
- Technology
- Sports
- Culture
- Major public events

Each current-events question must be fact-checked against a reliable, current source before the game.

If political subjects are used, questions should be strictly factual and nonpartisan.

## Difficulty

Target difficulty:

- **25% Easy**
- **50% Medium**
- **25% Challenging**

Avoid obscure trivia that only a specialist could reasonably know.

Even when a team does not immediately know an answer, participants should usually recognize the subject.

Because the audience spans several generations, rotate eras and topics rather than allowing one generation to dominate a long section of the game.

## Surprise Pastor Question

Include one **unannounced bonus question** based on the Pastor's sermon.

Players should **not be told beforehand** that they need to listen for a trivia answer.

The question should appear unexpectedly during the game, preferably around the middle.

Example placeholder:

> **What was the main theme of Pastor's sermon today?**

The exact question and accepted answer will be added once the sermon topic is known.

The host should confirm the accepted answer with Pastor before the game begins.

## Suggested Game Flow

1. Welcome everyone.
2. Explain teams, scoring, and how answers are submitted.
3. Begin mixed-category trivia.
4. Reveal answers after each question or small group of questions.
5. Show the scoreboard periodically.
6. Insert the surprise Pastor question around the middle of the game.
7. Continue through the remaining categories.
8. Show standings before the final question.
9. Play the final question.
10. Calculate scores and announce the winning team.

Short mixed rounds are preferred over one long block devoted entirely to a single category.

## Question-Writing Standards

Every question should:

- Have one clearly defensible answer or a documented set of acceptable answers.
- Be short enough to read comfortably from the back of the room.
- Avoid confusing or intentionally misleading wording.
- Include its category.
- Include its difficulty.
- Include the correct answer.
- Include a verification/source note when appropriate.

For current events and other time-sensitive facts, record both the source and verification date.

Before the event, review the complete question set for:

- Duplicate questions.
- Ambiguous wording.
- Incorrect answers.
- Category imbalance.
- Difficulty imbalance.
- Questions that are too obscure.
